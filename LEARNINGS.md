# Learnings

Raw material for the write-up and the demo site. Short bullets; numbers only where measured.

## Phase 0: planning (2026-10-26)

- None of the three search APIs (`amazon_search`, `ebay_search`, `shein_search`) documents a GTIN/UPC/EAN field. Matching across stores has to work from titles, brands and prices, or pay for a product-detail call per item.
- The engine schemas are close but not identical: eBay has `is_sponsored` and price ranges, Amazon has `brand` but no sponsored flag in its docs example, Shein returns both local and USD prices. "Same schema across engines" is a goal with visible seams.
- eBay's robots.txt disallows search result pages (`/sch/`) for all crawlers.
- Trial: 100 requests, and at most 20% of plan credits per hour.

## Phase 1: SearchApi client and cache (done 2026-10-26; 1 credit spent)

- Minitest 6 no longer ships `Minitest::Mock` or `stub`. The client takes its HTTP transport as an argument instead, so tests pass a fake that records what would go over the wire.
- Mutation check: 11 deliberately planted bugs (cache key, credit cap, secret leak, retries, ledger) were each caught by at least one test.

### First live response: `ebay_search`, q="adidas samba og" (2026-10-26, one response, 60 items)

Observations from a single response, not conclusions. Phase 3 (the hand-captured HTML) is where to check them.

- Timing: SearchApi reported 2.57 s (2.39 s fetching eBay, 0.18 s parsing); the full round trip from Belgrade measured 3.37 s.
- `search_parameters` echoes the defaults it applied (`ebay_domain: ebay.com`, `country: US`, `delivery_country: US`) and never the API key. `request_url` is `ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw=…`, the path eBay's robots.txt disallows.
- Undocumented fields: `subtitle` on 60/60 items (size and brand, e.g. "US M 11 + more · adidas · adidas Samba"), `tag` on 1/60. eBay results have no `brand` field, so `subtitle` is the best brand source.
- Documented fields never present: `seller`, `is_sponsored`, `images`, `watching`, `items_sold`, `authenticity`, `is_free_return` (0/60 each). Some of that data is there, but only as raw strings in `extensions` ("123 sold", "14+ watchers", "Authenticity Guarantee").
- `is_sponsored` is absent rather than `false`, so "not sponsored" and "unknown" look the same.
- Price ranges: 4/60 items have no `extracted_price`, only `extracted_price_range: {from, to}` (e.g. "$84.00 to $128.00"). The unified record needs a min–max price.
- Condition: 39 "Brand New", 15 "Pre-Owned", 6 "New (Other)". A third of the listings aren't new, so matching must look at condition.
- Style codes (two letters plus four digits, e.g. `JH5633`) appear in 19/60 titles.

## Phase 2: unified record and matching (2026-10-26)

- Fetched 8 queries from Amazon and eBay: 14 calls in one run, no errors. Latency per call: Amazon 2.1–14.6 s, eBay 2.5–8.0 s (one sample each, from Belgrade).
- Shein, four attempts: "adidas samba og" timed out in our client at 60 s (18:45 UTC), then got SearchApi 503 "We could not retrieve results for this search. Please try again later." after 90.5 s (18:50), then 200 "Shein didn't return any results." in 11.8 s (19:36). "nike dunk low panda" got the same 503 after 90.8 s (19:53), and so did the generic "women's puffer jacket" after 90.7 s (19:57). In total 5 attempts over about 70 minutes: 1 "no results", 4 failures, the three 503s all at about 90 s, which looks like SearchApi's deadline for Shein. No Shein listings, so no Shein converter. All five calls are in the ledger, and whether failures are billed is for the dashboard to say.
- At 19:58 UTC, status.searchapi.io said "All systems are operational". It reports the API and the landing page as a whole, with no per-engine status, so a Shein outage like this one would not show there. One customer, one hour, five calls: an observation, not a measurement of their reliability.
- Conversion: Amazon 448 listings became 441 products and 7 rejects (no price, only "more offers"); eBay 480 became 480 products. A test checks that every cached listing becomes a product or a reject with a reason, so nothing disappears silently.
- eBay: 215 of 480 listings (45%) are used. Condition has to be part of the record.
- eBay's misplaced shipping text holds across all 8 queries: 53 of 480 listings (11%).
- Amazon: `is_sponsored` is `true` on 64 of 448 listings and never `false`. `recent_sales` sometimes holds a badge instead ("Top Brand", "New on Amazon in past month").
- Brand: Amazon's field covers 392 of 441 products. eBay has no field: brands came from 357 titles and 6 subtitles, and 117 have none (mostly the generic queries, because our brand list only covers the queried brands).
- Style codes: 26% of eBay titles carry one (124/480) and 5% of Amazon titles (22/441). Sellers write codes; Amazon's catalogue titles mostly don't.
- Matching: the grouping uses 39 Amazon–eBay pairs (11 by style code, 28 by title). Precision: waiting for hand labels. First reading: style-code pairs look right, and some title pairs are clearly wrong (Dunk Low Twist vs Retro, a kids' "PS" 550 vs a men's, a leopard-print 501 vs the plain one).
- The variant problem, seen in real data: Amazon titles like "Mens Nike Dunk Low Retro" name no colorway. They are parent listings that cover many colours and sizes, so "the same product" is not well defined for them.
- No cross-store matches at all for "patagonia better sweater", "women's puffer jacket" or "crossbody bag".

## Phase 3: our own eBay parser against SearchApi (2026-10-26)

- Fetching (a deliberate decision, despite robots.txt): a visible Chrome with a fresh profile, driven by Ferrum, requested SearchApi's exact URLs 15 s apart. Pages 1 and 2 came back normally (200, about 2 MB, 60 listings each). The third got **403 "Error Page | eBay"**, and the script stopped as designed. One event cannot say which signal triggered it (automation, cookie-less profile, request rhythm or IP). Outcomes are in `fixtures/ebay_html/captures.jsonl`.
- eBay's markup (the "s-card" layout) has no meaningful class names for price, shipping or buying format: every row is a generic `su-styled-text` span. Auction cards with a Buy It Now option have two extra rows (bids and a second price). That is a likely mechanism for SearchApi's shipping errors: taking "the row after buying format" lands on "Buy It Now". The glued "Time left1d 8h" has a visible cause too: "Time left" is a separate hidden span, joined with no space. Both are hypotheses from the HTML, not from SearchApi's code.
- Our parser recognises rows by what they say, not where they sit. On the page used for the tests, every extracted shipping value is about shipping (49/49), against 88% plausible for SearchApi on the same query. It also parses the second, unseen page with 60 products and no rejects.
- The "Sponsored" label is hidden from text scrapers on purpose. It is an SVG image in a base64 `style` attribute, its text uses Cyrillic look-alike letters (`е`, `ѕ`) and an invisible U+2068, its accessible name goes through `aria-labelledby` references (some pointing at missing elements), and a reversed "derosnopS" sits in the text of all 62 cards. From the raw HTML every card looks sponsored. Our parser reports sponsored as unknown, and SearchApi returns no `is_sponsored` for eBay.
- Each page has two "Shop on eBay" placeholder cards linking to `/itm/123456`. SearchApi leaves them out (60 listings); so does our parser.
- Comparison on listings present on both sides (`bin/compare-ebay`): title 47/47, condition 47/47, price 43/43, price ranges 3/4 (the one difference is a price change between fetches) and subtitle 46/47. Shipping mostly differs, because of location: SearchApi fetched with US delivery and we fetched from Serbia, where the same listing can show "Shipping not specified", a different cost, or no shipping row at all. That is not a fair comparison.
- Only 20 and 27 of 60 listings appeared on both sides. The same URL returned largely different listings a few hours apart from another location.
- Normalisation choices that differ: SearchApi trims "+$6.99 delivery in 2-4 days" to "+$6.99 delivery" and leaves `extracted_shipping` out for free delivery; we keep the full text and use 0.0.
- One listing supports the row-shift hypothesis: for item 366518449313 our page says "+$4.99 delivery", while SearchApi's `shipping` holds "Located in United States".

## Phase 4: how clients look to a server (2026-10-26)

Measured on tls.peet.ws and bot.sannysoft.com, test services built to be probed. No store was contacted. Raw results, without IP addresses, are in `fixtures/fingerprints/` (`bin/fingerprints`, `bin/headless-signals`).

| Client | HTTP | User-Agent | JA4 | Ciphers / extensions | JA3 across connections |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ruby `Net::HTTP` (OpenSSL 3.5.5) | HTTP/1.1 | `Ruby` | `t13d3012_1d37bd780c83_89ab6efea773` | 30 / 12 | same twice |
| macOS `curl` 8.7.1 (LibreSSL) | h2 | `curl/8.7.1` | `t13d497h2_0d8feac7bc37_7395dae3b2f3` | 49 / 7 | same twice |
| Headless Chrome Dev 158 via Ferrum (CDP) | h2 | `HeadlessChrome/158.0.0.0` | `t13d1517h2_8daaf6152771_cb7bf5808d99` | 16 / 19 | 4 different hashes in 4 connections |
| Claude app's browser pane (Chromium 152, not headless) | h2 | `Chrome/152…` | `t13d1516h2_8daaf6152771_806a8c22fdea` | 16 / 18 | one sample |

- Chrome changes its TLS extension order on every connection, so its JA3 hash changes each time (4 hashes in 4 connections) while JA4, which sorts extensions first, stays the same. For Chrome, JA3 is useless as an identifier.
- At the TLS and HTTP/2 level, Ruby and curl look nothing like a browser: different cipher lists, extension sets, and for Ruby no HTTP/2 at all. Headless Chrome and the real Chromium share the same HTTP/2 fingerprint (`52d84b11…`). This is why tools like curl-impersonate exist.
- The TCP layer reflects the machine, not the client: every client showed TTL 45–47 and the same SYN signature. Changing the User-Agent does not change it, so a server can notice a "Windows" User-Agent arriving from a macOS or Linux TCP stack.
- bot.sannysoft.com: default headless Chrome failed 3 of 31 checks (the `HeadlessChrome` User-Agent, twice, and `CHR_MEMORY`). It passed WebDriver, plugins, WebGL and permissions. The real browser pane failed 1 (`HEADCHR_IFRAME`, a false positive). Page-level checks only catch the obvious; the network-level fingerprint separates a browser from an HTTP library far more reliably.
- Limits: one machine, one network, one run per client; the two Chromium builds are different versions (152 and 158), so their small JA4 difference may be the version, not headless mode. curl-impersonate was not tested (no Homebrew formula). No store was fetched with these clients.

## Phase 5, part 1: field health on SearchApi responses (2026-10-26)

- For each field in one response, the check measures how often it is filled and how often the value is plausible (`bin/field-health`). An empty response never passes, because zero listings is a signal of its own.
- Plausibility catches what fill rates miss. On the 16 saved responses, eBay's `shipping` fails plausibility in all 8 queries (85–93% against a 95% bar), because the field is filled, just sometimes with "Buy It Now". A fill-rate-only monitor would report it healthy.
- One false alarm: Amazon `brand` on "crossbody bag" is 67% filled against a 70% bar, because generic bags are often unbranded. One fixed threshold does not fit every query; canary queries need their own baselines.
- A simulated breakage (prices removed from a third of a real eBay response, like the Walmart fix in SearchApi's changelog) is caught. Test: `test_prices_emptied_on_a_third_of_listings_are_caught`.
- Phase 3 and the fix loop followed; see Phase 5, part 2.

## Phase 5, part 2: simulated layout changes and the agent fix loop (2026-10-26)

- Four simulated eBay layout changes, applied to both saved pages: renamed rows, a renamed title class, a "US $" price prefix, renamed cards. Detection compares each page with its own baseline and names the broken field in every case. A fifth change (renamed price class) is correctly ignored, because the parser reads rows by meaning.
- `bin/agent-fix` gave each change to `claude -p`, in its own git worktree, allowed to edit only the parser and run only its tests. The harness judged: tests pass, tests and fixtures untouched, no regressions on a held-out page no test reads. The agent was not told what changed.
- Result: **4 of 4 fixed on the first attempt.** 44–101 s each, 5–14 agent turns, 1–7 changed lines, $0.77–1.22 per fix as estimated by the CLI. Log: `data/agent_fix_log.jsonl`; diffs: `data/agent_fixes/`.
- Human review of the four passing fixes:
  - Three are what I would write: accept both the old and the new class name.
  - The price fix strips any two-or-three-letter code before "$" to keep the "$99.99" shape. A page in "AU $" would then be read as USD. The tests cannot see that; a reviewer must. Better: map "US $" to USD explicitly and reject unknown codes.
  - The agent wrote invented history into comments ("Since late October 2026 the price row carries a currency code"). The changes were simulated, but the prompt said the pages had been re-captured after eBay changed its markup, and the agent recorded that framing as fact.
  - One summary claimed "the linter is clean", although the agent was only allowed to run the tests. Checked afterwards: all four diffs are lint-clean, so the claim was true, but unverified when made.
- The fixes are not merged: they repair changes that never happened.
- Small sample: four scenarios, one run each, all mechanical renames or a format tweak. Real layout changes (restructured cards, content loaded by JavaScript, decoys) would be harder.

## Live search from the site (2026-10-26)

- Users can now search Amazon, eBay and Shein from the site. Results are saved; searching a saved term shows the saved results and asks "Run this search again?". Decisions: autocomplete is local (configured queries plus every query in the ledger), never SearchApi's autocomplete, which likely costs a credit per lookup. Live search works only when the site is started with `SEARCHAPI_LIVE=1`; users tick the stores (1 credit each); a re-run keeps the old results in `history/`.
- Spending credits from a browser needed its own guardrails: a POST with a CSRF token (`Rack::Protection::AuthenticityToken`), a redirect after the search so reloading cannot spend again, a confirmation with the credit count, and a 403 when live search is off.
- Stores are fetched in parallel threads, so a search takes as long as the slowest store, not the sum. Ruby's global VM lock does not stop threads overlapping on network waits.
- The credit-cap race from the Phase 1 review became real with parallel fetches. Reproduced twice before fixing: 3 calls on 2 credits with one client, and again with one client per request, which is how the site works. Fix: the reservation lives in the ledger, keyed by its file and shared by every client in the process. Across processes (the site and a CLI script at the same time) it is still not covered.
- Shein diagnostic: "dress", the most common Shein query there is, got the same 503 after 90.9 s (20:15 UTC). SearchApi's Shein engine was failing for every query: 6 attempts, 1 "no results", 5 failures. Not fixable from our side. The options are retrying later or reporting it to SearchApi with the timestamps; their 503s carry no search ID.
- **SearchApi's Shein parser misses products that are in the page** (2 of 2 cases). Each SearchApi response links the HTML it fetched (`search_metadata.html_url`). For both "no results" answers, that HTML is a normal Shein search page (`page_name: "page_search"`, about 1.9 MB), not a block page, and it contains matching products in an embedded `"products":[…]` JSON array: 20 listings for "adidas samba og", all Adidas and 19 of them Sambas (fetched in 10.9 s), and 20 Nike listings for "nike air 1", 9 Air Force 1 and 10 Air Jordan 1 (fetched in 74.5 s). (My first count said 15 and 12. It came from a text search that skipped titles with escaped quotes; the parser's exact count is 20 each.) Several carry style codes (`IF1945`, `B75807`, `KJ7447`, `CW2288-111`). SearchApi reported "Shein didn't return any results." both times. Its fetching works, slowly; its extraction does not, at least for these pages. The 503s after about 90 s may be the same failure on a timer, but that is unverified.
- That corrects an earlier assumption of mine: I took "no results" for Samba as plausible because Shein "mostly sells its own brands". Its marketplace does list branded sneakers.
- Each product has `goods_id`, `goods_name`, `salePrice`/`retailPrice` (with `usdAmount`), a brand badge (`premiumFlagNew.brand_badge_name`), `goods_img` and `cat_id`, which is enough for our own Shein converter.
- **Live console:** clicking search now starts a background run and opens a console page that streams each step as Server-Sent Events: credit reserved, the request (URL without the key), SearchApi's response with its own timings (store fetch vs parse), SearchApi's message, where the response was saved, and how each store finished. Every line comes from an event emitted by the code doing the work, nothing simulated. A reconnecting browser sends Last-Event-ID, so no line repeats; a crash in a run ends it with a visible line instead of a console that waits forever. Checked in the browser without spending (a run over already-saved stores), then with one real search (20:53 UTC, 1 credit): "nike air force 1" on eBay only. The console showed the request within a second, and the response lines arrived 4.3 s later: SearchApi fetched ebay.com in 3.18 s, parsed in 0.12 s, 60 results. The result was saved, and the search page then asked "Run this search again?".
- **Shein in the app, despite SearchApi's parser:** when SearchApi answers "no results", a second opinion downloads the page it fetched (free), counts the listings with our own parser, and says so in the live console. The catalog then shows those listings with a note that they were read by cross-shop from SearchApi's page. On the saved data, "adidas samba og" now has 136 listings (Amazon 56, eBay 60, Shein 20), and Shein listings join the matched groups. Parsing a 1.9 MB page takes about half a second, but only by scanning bytes: the first exploratory scan indexed the UTF-8 string by character, which makes Ruby walk from the start every time, and it ran for minutes.
- **The second opinion cannot cover failures.** A real Shein search through the live site ("new balance 550", 21:24 UTC) got SearchApi's 503 after 90.8 s. The console showed the request, then the 503 with SearchApi's message, then "finished". No second opinion ran, correctly: a 503 comes with no search id and no saved page. The workaround covers SearchApi's "no results" answers, not its failures. The Account API still reported 79 credits, so this failure was not billed either. Shein so far: 8 attempts, 2 "no results" on pages full of products (billed), 5 SearchApi 503s at about 90 s, 1 client timeout. Watching the run also showed two text flaws (raw JSON in the failure line, "fetching saved stores again" for a store with nothing saved), fixed test-first.
- **A live "hugo boss" search from the site** (21:36 UTC, all three stores, in parallel). Amazon answered in 6.3 s (SearchApi fetched amazon.com in 5.37 s), eBay in 10.5 s (9.62 s), Shein with SearchApi's 503 after 90.7 s. The Account API then reported 77 credits: 100 minus the 23 calls with HTTP 200, so the Shein failure was again not billed; `searches_this_hour` was 2, so failures do not seem to count toward the hourly limit either. Shein so far: 9 attempts, 2 "no results" (billed), 6 SearchApi 503s, 1 client timeout. All six 503s arrived between 90.4 and 90.9 s, whatever the query, which looks like a fixed deadline on SearchApi's side rather than a slow Shein (an inference; SearchApi's docs state no timeout). The run also exposed bugs 24 and 25, and two listings were rejected loudly rather than mis-recorded: an Amazon listing with no price, and an eBay condition our converter does not map yet ("New without box").
- **The demo recording** (22:23 UTC, "nike air max 90", all three stores, recorded by `bin/screenshots`). Amazon answered in 3.3 s (SearchApi fetched amazon.com in 2.2 s), eBay in 4.4 s, Shein with SearchApi's 503 after 90.9 s. The console showed the fixed credit lines in a real run for the first time: "30 of 90 used before this call", then "…, 1 more in flight" and "…, 2 more in flight". A separate "saphora" search (Amazon and eBay) ran from the site at the same moment. The Account API then reported 73 credits: 100 minus the 27 calls with HTTP 200. Shein so far: 10 attempts, 2 "no results" (billed), 7 SearchApi 503s, all between 90.4 and 90.9 s, and 1 client timeout.
- **Billing, measured** (20:58 UTC). The Account API (`GET /api/v1/me`) reported 79 of 100 trial credits remaining, and calling it twice did not change usage, so it is free. The ledger then had 26 calls: 21 with HTTP 200 and 5 failures (four SearchApi 503s, one client timeout). 100 − 21 = 79, so successful calls are billed, failed ones are not ("pay per success" holds), and the 2 saved-page downloads (`html_url`) were free. Shein's two "no results" answers count as successes: **2 credits for answers that missed products present on the page.** A fair question for SearchApi: should a parser miss be billed?
- The account reports `hourly_rate_limit: 200000`, not the 20 per hour I assumed for the trial from the pricing page's 20% rule. The pacing was more careful than needed, which is harmless.
- Two of the 26 calls were one search from the live site ("lacoste", Amazon and eBay, 20:55 UTC). If that holds, the five failures cost nothing; the dashboard will confirm.

## Bugs and surprises

Each entry: what happened, how we found it, the evidence, the fix.

1. **`Net::HTTP` silently retries a GET after a read timeout** (ours, prevented). Found by a test against a local TCP server: 2 requests arrived for 1 call. Fix: `max_retries: 0`. Test: `test_a_read_timeout_is_raised_without_retrying`.
   - Question for SearchApi: is a request billed if the client disconnects before the response? If so, this default makes every client-side timeout cost double.
2. **Cached JSON could only be read with a UTF-8 locale** (ours, real bug). Found on the first live call: with `LANG` unset, Ruby reads files as US-ASCII and `JSON.parse` failed on "’" (12 in the file) and "·" (101). Invisible in a normal terminal; it would break under cron, CI or Docker, which is where a scheduled check runs. The live path worked only because HTTP bodies are raw bytes. Fix: read cache files as UTF-8 explicitly. Test: `test_reads_utf8_even_when_the_process_locale_is_not_utf8`.
   - The same bug on the input side, found in review: with `LANG` unset, command-line arguments arrive labelled as raw bytes, so `bin/searchapi ebay_search q="women’s puffer jacket"` crashed while building the cache key. That happens before any request, so no credit was at risk. Fix: the CLI labels arguments as UTF-8 and rejects invalid ones by name. Tests run the CLI as a subprocess with no locale and live mode unset: `test/bin/searchapi_test.rb`.
   - Lesson: text crosses into a Ruby program at three edges here (files, HTTP bodies, command-line arguments), and each one needs an explicit encoding. Python has the same trap with `open()` using the locale, hence PEP 686.
3. **The linter silently skipped `bin/`** (tooling). Found by counting linted files: 15 instead of 16. Standard ignores `bin/*` by default. Fix: `.standard.yml` with `default_ignores: false`.
4. **Path traversal through the engine name** (ours, fixed). Found in review: `engine` goes straight into a file path, and the CLI takes it from the command line, so `bin/searchapi ../x q=y` pointed outside the cache folder. Fix: the cache accepts only `/\A[a-z0-9_]+\z/`, so every caller is protected, not just the CLI. Test: `test_an_engine_name_that_could_leave_the_cache_folder_is_rejected`.
   - Ruby trap: `^` and `$` match at every line break, not at the ends of the string, so `/^[a-z0-9_]+$/` accepts `"ebay_search\n../x"`. Checked by planting `^…$` in a copy: the newline case fails the suite.
5. **A shared constant could be changed in place** (ours, prevented). Ruby constants stop reassignment, not mutation. `Client::ENDPOINT` is a `URI` object, and a transport that set `.query` on it without copying would have changed where every later request goes. Fix: `ENDPOINT` is frozen. Test: `test_a_transport_that_mutates_the_endpoint_fails_loudly`. The first run of that test failed with `NameError` (a nested class can't see constants the outer test class gets through `include`), which is why you watch a test fail before trusting it.
6. **SearchApi's eBay `shipping` field sometimes holds other text** (theirs, observed once, unverified). In the first response, 7/60 items have "Buy It Now", "or Best Offer" or "Located in United States" in `shipping`. On auction listings the real cost ("+$14.95 delivery") sits in `extensions`. That looks like rows shifting by one when an auction card has an extra line. Also seen: "Free delivery" leaves `extracted_shipping` absent rather than `0.0`, and `buying_format` reads "0 bids · Time left1d 3h", with words glued together. To check against the HTML in Phase 3. Across all 8 queries: 53 of 480 listings (11%).
7. **Our timeout was shorter than SearchApi's own deadline** (ours, worked around). Shein: our client gave up at 60 s; on the next attempt SearchApi itself answered 503 at 90.5 s. With the shorter timeout we got a blind `Net::ReadTimeout` instead of their reason, and possibly paid for it. Fix: `SEARCHAPI_READ_TIMEOUT` for slow engines. Lesson: a client's timeout should be longer than the provider's.
   - SearchApi's Search History API (documented as free) would show whether a timed-out search finished on their side, but it answers 403 "You need an active subscription" on the trial.
8. **Grouping chained unrelated listings together** (ours, fixed). Joining every chain of matches (A~B and B~C puts A with C) merged 33 adidas listings into one group on real data. Fix: each eBay listing joins only its single best Amazon match. Test: `test_a_listing_joins_only_its_best_match_so_groups_do_not_chain`.
9. **A test broke when a column was added** (ours, test bug). The labels test edited the CSV by matching column positions as text; adding a link column shifted them. Fix: the test edits through the CSV parser, like a person in a spreadsheet would.
10. **The locale trap again, in a one-off command.** Reading the labels CSV without `encoding:` failed on "’". The library passes the encoding, so its tests were green. The fix belongs in the code that reads, not in remembering.
11. **Shein silently vanished from the search page** (ours, fixed). The catalog only reported missing engines among those with a converter; Shein has none, so it disappeared instead of showing as missing, which is "missing data looks like no results" again. Found by a web test. Fix: the catalog checks every engine in `config/queries.yml` and reports each missing one with its reason ("not cached", "no converter yet").
12. **Sinatra's ERB does not escape HTML by default** (prevented). Every title on the site comes from someone else's page. Fix: the erubi template engine with `escape_html: true`, so `<%= %>` escapes and raw output has to be asked for with `<%== %>`. Test: a `<script>` title renders as text.
13. **Every web test failed with "Host not permitted"** (a feature, not a bug). Sinatra 4.1 added host authorization against DNS rebinding: in development it only accepts requests addressed to localhost, `.test` names or IPs, and rack-test sends `Host: example.org`. Fix: the tests address `localhost`; the protection stays on. The site also binds to 127.0.0.1 only.
14. **The privacy filter redacted Chrome's version number** (ours, fixed). The sanitizer that strips IP addresses from saved fingerprints turned `HeadlessChrome/158.0.0.0` into `HeadlessChrome/[redacted ip]`: a four-part version number looks like an IPv4 address. Found on the first real run. Fix: only octets 0–255, and nothing directly after "/". Test: `test_version_numbers_are_not_mistaken_for_ip_addresses`.
15. **Our own health check flagged our parser** (not a bug). Shipping was filled on only 49 of 60 parsed listings, below the 90% bar. The page showed why: 11 listings have no shipping row at all for a visitor outside the US. The test expectation was wrong, not the parser. Lesson: a failing health check starts an investigation; it is not a verdict.
16. **Ferrum could not find Chrome** (environment). The installed app is the Dev channel: the executable inside `Google Chrome.app` is named `Google Chrome Dev`. Fix: look up the executable instead of hard-coding its name.
17. **The fix harness counted an expired login as three failed fix attempts** (ours, fixed). The `claude` CLI's login had expired, so every call returned an error after 6 seconds without doing anything, and the harness logged "attempt 1, 2, 3: failed". That number would have been false evidence in the write-up. Fix: an agent error stops the run and is logged as an error, never as an attempt. The misleading lines were deleted before any commit.
18. **A "no results" answer looked like an ordinary success** (ours, fixed). The third Shein attempt (19:36 UTC, 11.8 s) returned HTTP 200 with `status: "Success"` and `error: "Shein didn't return any results."` for "adidas samba og", which is plausible, since Shein sells mostly its own brands. The first two attempts (a timeout, then a 503) were therefore temporary. Caching it is right: it is a definitive, paid-for answer. But the client only looked at the HTTP status, so nothing showed that the paid call returned nothing. Fix: the ledger records SearchApi's `error` message (`api_error`), and the catalog shows it as the reason a store has no listings. The ledger line for that call was written before the fix and has no `api_error`.
19. **The agent CLI used the wrong login** (environment, fixed). Started from inside the Claude desktop app, `claude -p` inherited the app session's variables (`CLAUDECODE`, `CLAUDE_CODE_*`, `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL`), expected the app to supply its login, and failed with "OAuth session expired", even after a fresh terminal login. It also needs the Keychain, which the sandbox blocks. Fix: the harness removes those variables for the agent's process, and runs outside the sandbox. Found by narrowing it down: my guesses (expired login, then the Keychain alone) were each tested and ruled out first.
20. **Ruby 3 read a hash argument as keywords** (ours, test bug). `EngineTransport.new("amazon_search" => OK)` passed a hash without braces to a method that also takes `delay:`. Since Ruby 3.0 such a hash is treated as keyword arguments, so the positional argument was missing. Fix: braces around the hash. A Ruby 3 migration trap worth knowing.
21. **The site's footer claimed it could not spend credits** after live search was added (ours, fixed). A failing test's output showed the old sentence. Fix: the footer and "How it works" describe the new rules.
22. **A template could not see a module constant** (ours, fixed). The run page called `Console.line`, but Sinatra templates run in the app's scope, so Ruby looked for a top-level `Console` and raised `NameError`. Without a locale, the error page then also failed an encoding check: one bug, two symptoms. Running the test under both locales separated them. Fix: a helper inside the app (`console_line`), where the constant resolves.
23. **The saved-results site crashed with `NoMethodError: undefined method 'notes'`** (ours, process, fixed). A saved search opened on port 4567 returned a 500. The code was fine: the same URL returned 200 on port 4568. The 4567 server had started at 21:20, before `Catalog::Result` gained `notes` (22:10), and I had restarted only the live server after that change. In development Sinatra rereads templates from disk on every request but never reloads Ruby code, so a long-running server mixed new views with old classes. Fix: restarted it, then made the mix impossible: the site reads its templates once at start, like its code (`set :reload_templates, false`). A stale server is now stale as a whole: it still serves the old version, but consistently, and never crashes on half of a new one. The cost: a view change also needs a restart. Test: `test_templates_are_read_once_not_on_every_request`, which pins the environment to development, where Sinatra's default applies. Lesson: after a code change, restart every running server, not just the one being tested; a stale process fails in ways the tests cannot see, because every test run loads the current code.
24. **A fragrance gift set "matched" two used suits** (ours, fixed). A live "hugo boss" search produced one cross-store group: a BOSS 2-piece fragrance set on Amazon with two used Hugo Boss 2-piece suits on eBay, all through the style code `2-PIECE`. The style-code rule accepts a token of six or more characters that mixes letters and digits as a whole, so a hyphen can join a plain number to a plain word. Across all 1,228 saved Amazon and eBay titles, 20 style-code hits are like that (`3-PACK` 5 times, `2-PIECE` 3, `4-PIECE` 2, `600-DENIER`, `SIZE-10`, `XS-3XL` and others); only `2-PIECE` appears in both stores so far, and none of the 39 labelled pairs depends on one. Real codes have a letter-and-digit head (`DD1391-100`). Related miss: "DZ2794-001Women's" yields `DZ2794-001WOMEN`, which cannot match `DZ2794-001`. A style code was treated as strong evidence on its own; this is why the labelled sample matters. Fix, test first: the part before the hyphen must mix letters and digits on its own. Rerunning the matcher on every saved query under both rules, only "hugo boss" changed (its one group is gone); every other query kept the same pairs. Cost: `AG-LT23` ("Men's AG-LT23 Ultra Tennis Shoes"), possibly a real model number, is dropped too; the test says so. Still open: the glued `DZ2794-001WOMEN`.
25. **The console said every parallel call was the 28th** (ours, fixed). A three-store search printed "reserved 1 credit (27 of 90 used before this call)" three times. The cap itself counts calls in flight (tested), but the line reports only calls already recorded in the ledger, so it understates what is committed while calls run in parallel. Fix, test first: the ledger's `reserve` returns what was committed, read in the same locked step as the reservation (calls recorded, calls in flight), and the line reads "27 of 90 used before this call, 2 more in flight". Reading the count after reserving would race with calls finishing in between. The parallel test passed 20 runs of 20.
26. **Saved pages carried other people's data** (ours, fixed before the first push). Before making the repo public, I scanned every blob in the history, not just the current files. The API key and `.env` were never committed, but two things were: the eBay pages name six or seven sellers each with their feedback scores (in a block outside the listing cards), and each Shein page SearchApi saved has an `"ip"` field in Shein's page config, the address Shein saw for the client that fetched it (a different one per page). A first scan printed only its top 12 hits and hid the Shein addresses; a pattern test written for the purpose found them. Fix, test first: `CrossShop::TrimPage` keeps an allowlist (eBay's cards and the labels they point to, Shein's product list) and drops everything else, including tracking attributes and link queries, so whatever a page adds next stays out by default. `bin/trim-fixtures` applies it and keeps the originals in `private/`, which git ignores. A test fails if any fixture contains an IP address or a seller line, and the history was rewritten so no commit holds the originals. Pages shrank from about 2 MB to 0.2–0.34 MB. Proof that nothing the code reads changed: identical parser output on the original and trimmed pages for both stores, and under all five simulated eBay layout changes; the disguised "Sponsored" label (base64 SVG with Cyrillic letters, reversed text) is still in all 62 cards. The Shein speed test now puts 2 MB of multibyte text in front of the trimmed product list, because a small page would no longer catch the slow character indexing (measured on the padded page: about 69 s that way, against the test's 2 s limit).
